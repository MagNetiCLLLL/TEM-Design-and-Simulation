# Objective focus and STEM raster calibration

The relevant calibration is the specimen distance travelled per scan increment and the change in incidence angle across a scan. The tolerable focus excursion depends on the operating mode, field of view and accuracy required. A pivot displacement in millimetres alone does not establish either quantity.

## Documented instrument operation

* **FEI Titan.** The STEM focus control can hold the objective fixed and use illumination intensity, use the objective at fixed intensity, or use the objective for small steps and illumination for large ones. The eucentric-focus command restores the prealigned lens settings. The operator brings the specimen to that height with stage Z. These are documented options for this platform. The manual also describes storing STEM spatial calibrations at selected magnifications. [Titan TIA manual, sections 6.1 and 7.3](https://chenj.hosted.uark.edu/ChenLab/TEM/HRTEM-Titan/TIA.pdf#page=72)
* **Probe-corrected high-resolution STEM.** The Thermo Fisher S-CORR guide specifies coarse focus with specimen Z after corrector tuning and gives an ideal working defocus range of ±500 nm. This is a focus-setting recommendation near the reference alignment. It supplies neither an allowed axial pivot offset nor an atomic-resolution depth of field. [Thermo Fisher S-CORR user guide, Working with the tuned system](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/MSD/manuals/nanoport-tuing-cs-probe-s-corr-user-guide.pdf#page=4)
* **FEI Tecnai HM-STEM.** At eucentric height, the alignment first establishes the diffraction focus, centers the convergent diffraction pattern and then wobbles the beam shift in X and Y. The operator superposes the central disks and adjusts the perpendicular response for each direction. The guide relates this to scan scale, distortion, detector centering and focus across the frame. It treats diffraction-focus errors and the transient during line flyback as separate effects. The beam-tilt alignment checks that a tilted beam stays at the same specimen position. [Tecnai alignment manual, sections 5.3–5.5 and 12.5–12.6](https://www.dartmouth.edu/emlab/docs/fei_tecnai_f20_alignments_doc.pdf#page=65)
* **CEOS hexapole corrector.** Figure 2 identifies a selected field ray crossing the axis at the coma-free aperture plane near the objective front focal plane. Where available, the condenser minilens supplies TL11. Weighted DC offsets to the upper and lower scan coils provide the DP12 alignment deflection. Conjugacy of the selected ray does not specify an RMS waist for a finite source, or a focus-dependent raster-current table. [Müller et al., *Advancing the Hexapole Cs-Corrector for the Scanning Transmission Electron Microscope*, Fig. 2](https://www.ceos-gmbh.de/en/produkte/stemkorrektoren/dcor-ascor/Mueller2006a.pdf#page=3)

## Small changes about the reference focus

Let $u$ be a two-axis raster command. At the fixed specimen plane define $J=\partial\langle r\rangle/\partial u$ and $K=\partial\langle dr/dz\rangle/\partial u$. The angle is the direction of the beam centroid. The convergence semi-angle measures the spread of directions inside the probe. A telecentric scan has two usable lateral gains in $J$ and small $K$. A separate measurement determines the spot focus and its diameter. Over a small specimen-height change $h$ at fixed currents, the position Jacobian is $J(z+h)=J(z)+hK(z)+O(h^2)$. This explains reduced height sensitivity when $K$ is small. Varying the objective current at fixed height changes the fields and can change both matrices directly.

For a field-free, thin-lens illustration, a common angular kick $u$ at a pivot a distance $a$ before a positive lens of focal length $f$ gives a chief-ray slope $(1-a/f)u$ after the lens. A small mismatch generates a proportionally small angular error, largest at the edge of the chosen scan. This illustrative formula does not supply the focal length or allowable range of a magnetic immersion objective.

The specimen-plane scan scale can be checked against a grating of known pitch or calibrated crystalline lattice distances. The two principal scale factors and polar rotation of $J(I)J(I_0)^{-1}$ quantify changes relative to reference current $I_0$. A disk-stationarity test addresses angular purity and cannot by itself give nm/pixel. The diffraction relay must image the correct plane, with detector de-scan disabled or independently accounted for, for a detector displacement to be attributed to incident tilt. In magnetic immersion, the tracked mechanical angle, the canonical angular transfer and a projected disk position are distinct observables. [Reference nano-dimensional metrology by STEM](https://doi.org/10.1088/0957-0233/24/8/085001) · [Tecnai HM-STEM diffraction alignment](https://www.dartmouth.edu/emlab/docs/fei_tecnai_f20_alignments_doc.pdf#page=66)

For a conventional TEM camera image, specimen lengths are mapped by the objective, intermediate and projector lenses and the camera sampling. For a STEM raster image, specimen length per pixel comes from the specimen displacement per digital scan increment. A change in camera length affects the diffraction detector geometry and does not itself define the real-space raster scale. [JEOL imaging lens system](https://www.jeol.com/words/emterms/20121023.025059.php) · [Titan TIA manual, section 7.3](https://chenj.hosted.uark.edu/ChenLab/TEM/HRTEM-Titan/TIA.pdf#page=77)

## Fine focus on the 30 mrad branch

The original TL12 and Mini Condenser settings put the D95 minimum at the upper specimen surface with a 30.000 mrad physical pupil edge and D95 of 8.115 µm. At that reference current, the two lower scan channels were mixed with the two upper channels to minimize the differential mechanical incidence angle at the specimen. That four-channel mixing, the 100 µm C2 aperture and all other excitations were then held fixed. The position and slope measurements use the centroids of the original surviving packets, differenced for positive and negative scan commands. The independently sampled D95 minimum uses the actual fields over a short vacuum extension about the specimen plane. Positive axial offsets point downstream.

Both 0.0375 and 0.01875 mm maximum integration steps gave these same focus brackets. The 10 nm spacing of the axial observations gives a one-step bracket on either side of each tabulated estimate. Of 32,768 original particles, the same 30,396 reached the specimen in all six calculations. The finest integrations gave edge angles from 29.99988 to 30.00008 mrad. The maximum residual mechanical tilt gain at either perturbed setting was 1.36 × 10⁻⁶ µrad per µrad of scan command. Independent half-amplitude perturbations reproduced the scan-position responses.

Pixel scale here means the local derivative of specimen displacement per scan increment. The immersion-field mechanical-angle proxy leaves the downstream diffraction optics outside this calculation. ## Larger excursion on the downstream waist-matched branch

The following sensitivity test keeps the Mini Condenser, TL12, all four AC winding combinations, the 100 µm C2 aperture and the specimen height fixed. Each objective current uses its own nonlinear material-field solution with the same winding count and geometry. All three runs transport the same 32,768 archived positive-current gun-exit packets at 300 keV, starting at column z = 450 mm. The 30,409 surviving packets are common to the three runs. The reference calibration in this table is the downstream waist-matched branch. Its pupil rim is 0.224 mrad and its specimen D95 is 269.24 µm.

The common pivot minimizes the sum of squared position responses to both scan commands along the column. After either perturbation, the separately fitted X/Y minima differ by about 14 µm and even the best common plane retains 0.096 nm/µrad of maximum centroid motion. Its coordinate is consequently an approximate pivot. Independent launches at half the drive amplitude verify the residual. The ±0.1000 A excursion is a model sensitivity test. The simulated metre-scale column registration, uncalibrated windings and near-axis static scans cannot set a hardware focus tolerance. ## Applying the calibration to this column

At the nominal Nanoprobe setting, establish the requested TL12 waist and measure the downstream virtual pivot and the two-lens front focus independently. When studying a focus excursion, keep the four scan-current combinations fixed first. Record the D95 through-focus curve, physical pupil-edge semi-angle, specimen-position Jacobian and scan-dependent incidence angle at the fixed specimen height. A detector-space check additionally requires the post-specimen diffraction optics. For an intended field of view, repeat at the field edges to identify nonlinear distortion or loss of probe quality. This gives an observable basis for retaining a scan calibration over a focus range or recalibrating it for another operating mode. A Si[110] length reference can check the real-space pixel scale independently once the probe resolves the relevant spacings.
