# Cold FEG gun-lens field, placement and current acceptance

The simulated gun lens has the expected electrostatic force signs for the electrode potentials applied. Its distance from the emitter, the voltage on the nearby aperture and the relation between the operator's gun-lens setting and the physical electrode voltage remain uncalibrated for a commercial X-CFEG. These quantities determine where the source is imaged and how much current enters the column.

## What the instrument operator controls

Thermo Fisher documents coarse X-CFEG gun-lens settings of 1 through 8, with fine increments of 0.1. Lowering the number delivers more current through the column. Its beam-current readout is measured at the X-CFEG entrance aperture and represents the beam available ahead of condenser-aperture losses. The extractor has a separate voltage readout and regulates emission at the tip. The note supplies no conversion from a gun-lens number to a cathode-referenced voltage. [Thermo Fisher X-CFEG application note, p. 3](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/MSD/Application-Notes/xcfeg-application-note-an0171.pdf#page=3)

FEI's Titan material puts the electrostatic gun lens downstream of the extractor. It forms the source image used by the C1 lens. FEI's Tecnai alignment guide illustrates how moving the crossover farther upstream of a fixed beam-defining aperture can decrease transmitted current. This is a mechanism for current variation even when electrons clear the gun-lens electrode. The guide's example uses the C2 aperture on that particular column. [FEI Titan Training Material, module 1](https://emfacility.science.oregonstate.edu/sites/emfacility.science.oregonstate.edu/files/2025-09/Titan%20training%20manual_0.pdf) · [FEI Tecnai alignment guide](https://www.dartmouth.edu/emlab/docs/fei_tecnai_f20_alignments_doc.pdf)

## Direction and extent in the joint model

The positive z axis points downstream. The code solves for potential relative to the tip. The electric field equals the negative potential gradient. An electron's electric force points opposite to the field. The former +1.2 kV setting referenced to the extractor gives +5.2 kV referenced to the tip at +4.0 kV extraction. A distinct +1.2 kV **tip-referenced** setting is 2.8 kV below the extractor. These settings produce different entrance fields. [Current assembly and voltage reference](../../../../configs/instruments/gun/FEG.toml) · [Recorded reference change](../../../references/fei-cold-feg-extractor-2026-11-07.md)

| Position and observable | +1.2 kV relative to tip | +1.8 kV relative to tip | Electron force |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| z = 8 mm, axial field on axis | +201 kV/m | +155 kV/m | Upstream, retarding a forward electron |
| z = 18 mm, radial field at r = 0.10 mm | +3.71 kV/m | +3.60 kV/m | Radially inward |
| z = 22 mm, axial field on axis | −790 kV/m | −788 kV/m | Downstream, accelerating a forward electron |

The previous operating points give a direct sign check at the same locations. At z = 8 mm the axial field is −101.8 kV/m at +5.2 kV, −109.3 kV/m at +5.3 kV, and +290.2 kV/m at +17.13 V. Lowering the electrode to the tuned value changes the upstream gap from accelerating to retarding at this location. At z = 18 mm and r = 0.10 mm the radial electric field points outwards in all three cases (+2.98, +2.97 and +3.93 kV/m, respectively), giving an inward electron force. Electrons also traverse the low-voltage case at lower longitudinal momentum. The observed first image at 24 mm is specific to that working point and geometry. [Working-point field samples](electrode-field-control.json) · [First-image verification](README.md)

A separate static boundary-control calculation increased the lens voltage by 600 V and held **each accelerator ring** at its original potential. Near the gun aperture at 24 mm, the on-axis field changed by +25.0 kV/m. With the model's ordinary coupled divider the same change at 24 mm was +2.61 kV/m. At 8 mm the two responses were both −45.4 kV/m. This distinguishes the direct change in the lens field from the contribution of the divider. ## Can the gun lens itself intercept current?

An electron hitting the metal around the lens bore can contribute to its collected current. Electrons clearing that bore can still encounter a limiting aperture as their inclination or crossover changes. A cold-FEG patent illustrates primary electrons hitting the extractor, followed by further selection at another plate. A modified JEOL cold-source experiment identifies limiting apertures at the extractor and source exit and estimates about 3% maximum transmission in its photoemission experiment. Those are distinct mechanisms and instruments. [FEI US8736170B1, Fig. 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8736170B1/en) · [Experimental study, p. 3](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/77543/1/1-s2.0-S0304399125000579-main.pdf#page=3)

Each of the following trials launched 2,048 distinct, positive-current particles from the unchanged effective-source distribution with a prescribed total current of 15.00 µA. The original source IDs, launch positions and angles match the first 2,048 members of the 16,384-particle archive. The smaller run uses the source's own weights summing to unity before transport. It uses the existing common magnetic field and the 4 mm gun aperture throughout.

| Physical gun-lens voltage relative to tip | Greatest sampled particle radius inside lens | Greatest radius at gun aperture midplane | Lens current intercepted | Gun aperture current intercepted | Gun-exit current at 450 mm |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| +1.2 kV | 1.18 µm | 0.97 µm | 0 µA | 0 µA | 15.00 µA |
| +1.8 kV | 1.15 µm | 1.03 µm | 0 µA | 0 µA | 15.00 µA |

All 2,048 particles survived at each setting, with effective count 2,048 in both cases. The gun-lens bore radius is 4,000 µm and the gun-aperture radius is 2,000 µm. The existing source puts the full prescribed emission into a narrow set of outgoing trajectories. The largest sampled launch-direction slope in the 16,384-particle archive is 3.00 mrad at the emission plane. The measured instrument's broad emission and angular current distribution have yet to be supplied. Primary mechanical stops already identify the lens electrode separately from the extractor and aperture. In the gun tracer the lens bore uses accepted step endpoints. Grazing-impact studies would require a swept-intersection check. The several-thousand-fold clearance in these two runs is far larger than a grazing uncertainty. [Current ledger and field samples](electrode-acceptance.json) · [Gun tracer](../../../../src/temsim/optics/electron_gun/tracing.py)

The tuned +17.13 V setting in the parent study images the prescribed source at the chosen 24 mm foil. It remains a model-specific diagnostic. Establishing the hardware response calls for the source angular current distribution, calibrated bias and reference for at least two operating settings, and the electrical boundary at the gun aperture or exit anode. With extractor and downstream controls fixed, separate readings of emission, gun-region BCM, any available lens-electrode collection current and specimen current would locate the first stage where current changes. The joint field and primary-particle losses should then predict current versus setting. [24 mm source-image study](README.md)
