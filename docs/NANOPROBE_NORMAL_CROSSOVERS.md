# Nanoprobe Normal crossover and conjugate-plane guide

In three-condenser STEM Nanoprobe Normal, the beam must form **two intermediate source images after the C3 lens**. The first is between TL22 and TL21. The second is after the lower AC scan winding and before the Mini Condenser. The upper objective then brings the beam to its terminal focus at the specimen upper surface.

## Where to count a crossover

The simulator and Müller et al. call the round transfer lens **above the scan coils TL12** (`probe_tl12_lens`). The **Mini Condenser below the coils serves as TL11** (`mini_condenser`). The tuning request calls these physical elements “TL11” and “TL12 (Mini Condenser)”, respectively. The table follows the hardware in beam order and uses the simulator names. Report the angular distribution of transmitted current separately. The aperture may transmit the entire illuminated beam.

Track the same positive-current source particles and check that the current-weighted transverse radius has a minimum, with both transverse directions changing from contraction to expansion in the same axial neighbourhood. A crossing visible only in the x projection or a one-directional line focus is insufficient evidence. The existing chain report inventories ADL→TL22 and TL21→TL12. ## Conditions to preserve while tuning

1. **Hexapole and pupil conjugacy.** TL22 and TL21 image the HP2 field midplane onto the HP1 field midplane. The output relay (TL12 and the TL11 Mini Condenser) matches HP1 to the illumination-side **coma-free aperture plane of the objective**. Locate that optical plane at the operating objective current. Test HP2→HP1, HP1→pupil and HP2→pupil with ±1 µrad launch-angle perturbations in x and y at fixed launch position. At a conjugate plane the first-order change in landing position approaches zero for both input directions. Record the residual in nm/µrad and the image magnification and rotation. The symmetric HP pair uses unit-magnitude imaging and matched field azimuth as a design reference. The HP1→pupil magnification sets the scale of the correction. The TL22/TL21 mechanical midpoint, currently 1208.15 mm, is only a reference for locating the core's optical symmetry. [Relay measurement](development/cescor_direct_probe_20261121/relay-conjugacy.md) · [CEOS on symmetry and three-lobe aberration](https://www.ceos-gmbh.de/en/produkte/residualsCEXCOR)
2. **Post-scan waist, AC pivot and lens-pair front.** Use TL12 to place the static source waist after the lower scan winding. Independently locate the pure-tilt pivot with signed ±1 µrad x and y scan commands. At the pivot the change in beam position should vanish and the change in angle should follow the command. Measure its offset from the Mini Condenser and upper objective front at the final objective current. The static source has a finite image distance to the specimen. In one canonical transverse meridian, imaging a point onto the specimen requires the transfer coefficient B to vanish. A front focal plane sends a fan from a point into parallel rays, so D vanishes at the field-free output in the same convention. A nonsingular transfer cannot have both B and D vanish in the same column of its matrix. Check source imaging, scan calibration and the location of the objective pupil independently. The scan pair steers the centroid. A stigmator can focus the two transverse directions differently. [Scan calibration](development/cescor_direct_probe_20261121/scan-focus-calibration.md) · [Three-plane comparison](development/cescor_direct_probe_20261121/post-scan-crossover.md)
3. **Specimen focus.** After relay alignment, use only the objective supply for routine fine focus. Recheck the three planes and pupil conjugacy at the final current. Bracket the minimum of D95 on both sides of the specimen upper surface and report its offset. At the surface also report the two-dimensional landing distribution, RMS radius and current in a fixed on-axis disk of stated radius. These observables can reach their optima at different depths. [Specimen through-focus data](development/cescor_direct_probe_20261121/evidence/material-relay/post-scan-objective-focus/surface-focus.json)

## How HP1 and HP2 affect convergence

For a centred beam in an ideal pure hexapole, the leading transverse magnetic field is quadratic in distance from the axis. Its on-axis linear focusing term is zero. A paraxial beam can therefore pass through either HP midplane already converging or diverging under the round-lens fields. At finite aperture the hexapoles deflect rays by different amounts and directions across the pupil. With matched relay magnification, rotation and excitation, the pair can supply negative spherical aberration to compensate the positive contribution of the objective. Changing HP excitation can consequently change the threefold pattern, D95 and the best-focus position. Miscentring can also produce an effective quadrupole term. [Hartel et al., hexapole field model, §2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304399119302347) · [Müller et al., corrector design](https://www.ceos-gmbh.de/en/produkte/stemkorrektoren/dcor-ascor/Mueller2006a.pdf)

To attribute a measured change to the pair, compare its operating excitation with a second joint finite-element solve at zero HP1/HP2 current. Keep the installed pole pieces, sleeves, return paths, material curves, other winding currents and physical apertures identical. Relaunch the same particles from the effective source in each field. Compare paraxial transfer, static waist positions and specimen landings at the same objective setting before allowing refocus.

## Reference measurement for the present geometry

The archived material-field run used 16,384 original particles at zero scan, with the C1 lens at −30 A and the C2 lens at +16 A. The two downstream waists are **1209.153 mm** between TL22 and TL21 and **1422.987 mm** after the lower scan winding. Only the first falls inside the shaded HP2–TL12 corrector body. The earlier full-column waists are at 524.047 and 787.096 mm. [Saved working point and ray measurements](development/cescor_direct_probe_20261121/evidence/material-relay/post-scan-objective-focus/working-point.json) · [Dense count inside the corrector](development/cescor_direct_probe_20261121/evidence/material-relay/post-scan-objective-focus/corrector-crossover-count.json)

At the final objective current the AC pivot is at 1423.004 mm and the lens-pair front is at 1422.855 mm. The post-scan waist is 17 µm upstream of the pivot and 131 µm downstream of the front. The measured surface D95 is **575.47 nm**. The ±0.4 mm through-focus window did not bracket a D95 minimum. The intermediate count is established for this run. Specimen focus, the working aperture angle and the pupil conjugacies at this field setting still need their own evidence.
